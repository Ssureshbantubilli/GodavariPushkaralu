What is Pushkaralu?

Pushkaralu (also known as Pushkaram) is a sacred Hindu festival celebrated along the banks of India's major rivers. The festival occurs once every 12 years for each river, and is observed when the planet Jupiter (Brihaspati) transits into a specific zodiac sign associated with that river.

For the Godavari River, Pushkaralu is held when Jupiter enters Simha Rashi (Leo). Devotees believe that taking a holy dip in the river during this period washes away sins and grants liberation (moksha).

The Godavari River

The Godavari, known as the "Dakshina Ganga" (Ganges of the South), is the second-longest river in India after the Ganges. It originates in Trimbakeshwar, near Nashik in Maharashtra, and flows eastward for about 1,465 km before emptying into the Bay of Bengal near Rajahmundry, Andhra Pradesh.

Revered in Hindu scriptures including the Ramayana and several Puranas, the Godavari is believed to have divine powers of purification. The river is also called "Vridha Ganga" (the elder Ganges) and "Gautami" — after the sage Gautama.

Mythological Significance

According to Hindu mythology, the sage Gautama performed severe penances at Trimbakeshwar, which pleased Lord Shiva. As a boon, Shiva caused the Ganges to flow down to the place where Gautama was meditating, giving rise to the Godavari. Hence, the river is also called "Gautami Ganga."

The Ramayana states that Lord Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana spent a portion of their exile in the Panchavati region on the banks of the Godavari near Nashik. Lord Rama also crossed the Godavari to reach Bhadrachalam, which is now one of the most important pilgrimage centers on the river.

Adi Pushkaram and Anthya Pushkaram

The festival spans 12 days in total. The first day is called Adi Pushkaram and is considered the most auspicious for taking a holy bath. The last (12th) day is called Anthya Pushkaram and marks the conclusion of the festival. Both these days attract the largest number of pilgrims.

Rituals Performed

During Pushkaralu, devotees perform several sacred rituals, including:

  • Snanam — Taking a sacred bath in the river
  • Pitru Tarpanam — Offering water to ancestors for their peace
  • Danam — Charitable giving to the needy and priests
  • Japa — Chanting of mantras and prayers
  • Homa — Ritual fire offerings
  • Kathadanam — Donating books and religious texts
  • Deepa Daanam — Lighting sacred lamps by the river

2027 Festival

The next Godavari Pushkaralu is expected to be held in 2027 when Jupiter transits into Simha Rashi. Both the Andhra Pradesh and Telangana state governments are making extensive preparations to welcome crores of pilgrims. Mega infrastructure projects including ghat renovations, transportation networks, and digital facilities are being planned.
